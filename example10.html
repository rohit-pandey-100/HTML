  • Uttar Pradesh

Uttar Pradesh reports one of the highest numbers of cancer cases in India because it is the country's most populous state. Common cancers include oral cancer, breast cancer, cervical cancer, and lung cancer. Tobacco use, poor awareness, delayed diagnosis, and limited access to healthcare in some rural areas contribute to the high cancer burden. Expanding cancer screening programs and improving access to quality healthcare can help reduce the number of cancer cases.


The state government has been working to improve cancer care by expanding hospitals, diagnostic centers, and awareness campaigns. Several government and private hospitals now provide chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and cancer surgeries. Health experts emphasize that avoiding tobacco, maintaining a healthy diet, exercising regularly, and undergoing routine medical check-ups can significantly reduce the risk of cancer. Early diagnosis remains one of the most effective ways to improve survival rates.

  • Maharashtra

Maharashtra is one of India's most developed states and has a large urban population, leading to a high number of cancer cases. Major cities such as Mumbai, Pune, and Nagpur have excellent cancer treatment facilities and research centers. The most commonly diagnosed cancers include breast cancer, oral cancer, lung cancer, colorectal cancer, and prostate cancer. Rapid urbanization, pollution, unhealthy diets, stress, tobacco use, and alcohol consumption contribute to the increasing number of cases.


The state is home to several renowned cancer hospitals that offer advanced treatments such as immunotherapy, targeted therapy, robotic surgery, and radiation therapy. Government health programs encourage regular cancer screening, especially for breast and cervical cancer among women. Public awareness campaigns continue to educate people about healthy lifestyles and the importance of early detection, which greatly increases the chances of successful treatment.

  • West Bengal

West Bengal reports a significant number of cancer cases every year due to its large population and lifestyle-related risk factors. Oral cancer, lung cancer, stomach cancer, liver cancer, and breast cancer are among the most common cancers in the state. Tobacco use in both smoking and chewing forms, along with dietary habits and environmental pollution, plays a major role in increasing cancer risk.


The state has established specialized cancer hospitals and medical colleges that provide diagnosis, surgery, chemotherapy, and radiation treatment. Awareness campaigns encourage people to avoid tobacco products, eat nutritious food, exercise regularly, and seek medical attention if they notice unusual symptoms. Improving healthcare access in rural areas and promoting regular health screenings are essential steps toward reducing cancer-related deaths.

  • Bihar

Bihar has experienced a steady increase in cancer cases over the past few years. Oral cancer is particularly common because of the widespread use of chewing tobacco, gutkha, and paan. Other frequently diagnosed cancers include breast cancer, cervical cancer, lung cancer, and esophageal cancer. Limited healthcare infrastructure in some districts often results in delayed diagnosis, reducing the chances of successful treatment.


The government and healthcare organizations are working to improve cancer awareness, establish more diagnostic centers, and provide affordable treatment services. Campaigns promoting tobacco cessation, healthy eating habits, vaccination against cancer-related infections such as HPV, and regular health check-ups are helping reduce the disease burden. Increasing awareness about the early symptoms of cancer remains one of the state's highest priorities.

  • Tamil Nadu

Tamil Nadu is recognized for having one of the strongest healthcare systems in India and one of the most organized cancer registration programs. As a result, the state reports a high number of diagnosed cancer cases. Breast cancer, cervical cancer, thyroid cancer, colorectal cancer, lung cancer, and oral cancer are among the most commonly reported cancers. The higher number of reported cases also reflects better medical facilities and more effective cancer detection systems.


Tamil Nadu has several world-class cancer hospitals and research institutions that provide comprehensive cancer care, including surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, and palliative care. The state government actively promotes free cancer screening camps, vaccination programs, and public awareness initiatives. These efforts have improved early diagnosis, increased survival rates, and enhanced the quality of life for cancer patients. Tamil Nadu continues to serve as a model for cancer prevention, treatment, and research in India.
